# MRM requirements

Tracked requirements for the MRM stack. Every requirement needs a verification method.
Numeric limits marked **TBD** are proposals and must be confirmed with the controller team (MABX).
The first values are deliberately conservative (Autoware defaults of our `autoware_launch` fork) and will be sharpened later.

## Requirements

| ID | Requirement | Rationale | Verification | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MRM-001 | **No lateral jump at the switch.** At every cycle, the distance between the ego pose (rear-axle center) and the MRM trajectory (perpendicular distance to the polyline of `/mrm/trajectory`) is <= 0.05 m in normal driving and <= 0.15 m in extreme driving. These are the lateral tracking errors of our controller, so the switch never asks for more than the controller handles anyway. | The switch from the nominal trajectory to the MRM trajectory is sudden (no blending between the two). An offset of the MRM trajectory relative to the ego is therefore a step in the controller's lateral error, and causes large steering angle, yaw acceleration and lateral acceleration. | Scenario KPI in every standby cycle and at the trigger, with the nominal driving off the lane center. Drive check: compare `/localization/kinematic_state` with the raw A-ROX pose in the local map frame (what the MABX uses). | verified in the planning simulator (nominal 0.8 m off the lane center: 0.000 m, every cycle). The MABX uses the raw A-ROX pose in the local map frame, Autoware uses the EKF output of the same pose: the difference still has to be measured on a drive |
| MRM-002 | **Smooth start of the MRM trajectory.** The heading at the first point equals the ego heading. Tolerance (conservative estimate, **TBD**): <= 1 deg normal, <= 3 deg extreme (the lateral errors 0.05 m / 0.15 m over the 3.0 m wheelbase). The path from the ego pose to Autoware's planned path stays below conservative limits (**TBD**, from our Autoware defaults: lateral acceleration <= 1.0 m/s2, steering angle rate <= 11.5 deg/s at low speed and 10.5 deg/s up to 20 m/s). Yaw acceleration limit **TBD**. | Same as MRM-001: the controller must not see steps in heading or curvature when it takes over. | Unit tests of the geometry; KPI plots of lateral acceleration, yaw rate and steering rate after the trigger. | partly: heading equals the ego heading by construction; the start of the path is sized for 0.5 m/s2 (simulator: 0.49 m/s2). Yaw acceleration is not evaluated, the limits are not agreed |
| MRM-003 | **Pose reference point is the rear-axle center on the ground.** The pose the MRM stack uses (`/localization/kinematic_state`) refers to the rear-axle center projected on the ground (`base_link`). | A constant offset of the reference point adds to the error of MRM-001 and cannot be found by unit tests. | Configuration: the A-ROX lever arm is set to the ground projection of the rear-axle center (confirmed by the team), `autoware_launch` relays the GNSS pose without a transform. Drive check as in MRM-001. | configuration confirmed; drive check open |
| MRM-004 | **MRM trajectory is available.** Whenever the nominal system drives, the age of the newest valid `/mrm/trajectory` at the MABX is <= 0.1 s. If the stack cannot guarantee a valid trajectory, an old trajectory is not published (the stream stops, reason on `/mrm/status`). The MABX handles a stopped or old stream (its own logic). | The MABX can switch at any time, also right after a re-route or when localization or planning have a hiccup. | Scenarios with re-routing, localization dropout, planning restart: measure the interval between valid trajectories, and the latency from the planner to the UDP packet. | partly: invalid input leads to no output plus status. Simulator (`reroute_gap`, drive across the route refresh): longest interval between trajectories 53 ms, trajectory age at the receiver 3 ms. The bridge and UDP latency to the MABX are not measured |
| MRM-005 | **The stop point is fixed after the trigger.** After the trigger, the stop point of `/mrm/trajectory` does not move more than **TBD** (proposal: 0.5 m) along the path. | Without it the stop point can creep while braking, because every cycle starts from the current state. | Closed-loop unit test (exists), scenario KPI: stop point of the trajectory over time after the trigger. | verified in unit test and in all trigger scenarios of the simulator: stop point of the trajectory moves 0.01 to 0.12 m after the trigger. Tolerance **TBD** |
| MRM-006 | **Comfortable stop.** Planned deceleration <= 1.0 m/s2 and jerk <= 1.0 m/s3 (Autoware comfort values of our fork; Autoware's absolute limits are 2.5 m/s2 and 1.5 m/s3). Values **TBD**. | Defines "comfortable". | KPI from the scenarios: planned and measured deceleration and jerk. | planner parameters set to these values. Simulator, steady 8 m/s: planned 1.005 m/s2, measured peak 1.13 m/s2 and jerk 1.1 m/s3. Trigger while the nominal accelerates: measured 1.6 m/s2 / 3.7 m/s3 (step at the switch) |
| MRM-007 | **The trajectory is complete and ends in standstill.** The MRM trajectory always reaches the stop point plus a margin and ends with speed 0 (the MABX does a full stop with emergency brake at the last index). If Autoware's path is too short for the stop, this is reported (`path_short` in `/mrm/status`). | An MRM trajectory that ends while the car is still moving causes an emergency brake. | Unit test (ends at 0, exists), `path_short` check in the scenarios. The bridge sends a window of about 3 s of planned motion, so the end of the trajectory is only visible to the MABX in the last 3 s of the stop (to be checked together with the MABX). | partly: planner side done; all scenarios: every trajectory ends at speed 0, final speed 0.000 m/s. The 3 s window of the bridge towards the MABX is open (question 2) |
| MRM-008 | **The MRM finds a suitable path, also around obstacles.** The MRM trajectory follows Autoware's planned path, including avoiding a parked car on the side of the lane. Autoware's behavior modules therefore have to run without an approval (`enable_all_modules_auto_mode`). | An MRM that only follows the lane center would stop in front of a parked car, or hit it. | Scenario with a dummy parked obstacle on the right side of the lane (planning simulator), trigger before and during the avoidance. | partly verified in the simulator (`enable_all_modules_auto_mode` is `true` there; the default in `autoware_launch` is `false`). Parked car that fits past inside the lane (`parked_car_avoidable`): Autoware's own plan shifts 0.42 m to the left before the car, the MRM trajectory follows it (shift 0.41 m) and passes with 0.73 m clearance, the stop ends after the car. Car that does not fit with the team's avoidance margins (`parked_car_blocking`): Autoware plans a stop, the MRM stops 17.5 m in front. **Open:** the part of the path from the ego pose to Autoware's path is not checked against objects. A stop that needs more than 2.5 m/s2 is physically not possible (reported in `/mrm/status`), an earlier test with such a case ended in contact |

## Design decisions (not requirements)

These are choices of the implementation, they can change without changing a requirement.

- **Switch:** sudden, without blending between nominal and MRM trajectory. The MRM trajectory starts at the ego pose (MRM-001).
- **Path:** the MRM geometry is Autoware's planned path (`/planning/trajectory`: lane following, avoidance, lane change), not the lanelet centerline. `mrm_stop_planner` only changes the start (from the ego pose to the path, cubic lateral offset) and the speed. The lanelet map is used by `mrm_route_generator` to pick the lanes of the route.
- **Lateral behavior:** Autoware's standard behaviors decide (lane following, static obstacle avoidance with the team's tuned margins, lane change). The MRM does not add its own lateral logic. It only connects the ego pose to Autoware's path (MRM-001/002).
- **Route:** generated automatically along the current lane, frozen after the trigger.
- **Autoware speed:** Autoware's planned speeds are not used (in hot standby they lag or lead the real speed). Only stop points in Autoware's plan (obstacles) are used: the MRM stops there with up to 2.5 m/s2 and 1.5 m/s3 if the comfortable stop would be too long. A stop that needs more than that is not demanded, it is reported on `/mrm/status`.
- **Stop profile:** latched at the trigger, jerk-limited.

## Open questions

1. Heading tolerances (MRM-002): the conservative estimates above, to be replaced by values from the controller team.
2. MRM-008: check the transition from the ego pose to Autoware's path against objects (for the case that the nominal drives where Autoware would not drive, e.g. closer to an obstacle than Autoware's margins allow).

## Decided

- The stop profile starts with acceleration 0, a high jerk at the switch is not wanted (team decision).
- If the trajectory window sent to the MABX ends in standstill at the last index, the car just stands (MRM-007).
- Autoware's standard behaviors, not own logic, decide the lateral geometry of the MRM path.
